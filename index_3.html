The fund assembles 87 acres at a blended $4.85/SF and carries it on a bridge loan. The city's TIF district issues a $102.4M bond serviced entirely by future tax increment; the bond funds the public assets (YFAC, parking garage, infrastructure), reimburses the Costco land at fair market value, and escrows a per-lot incentive program. Lot-level construction debt plus recycled bond and disposition proceeds fund most of the vertical build, so limited partners wire $24.7M in total. Thirteen income-producing lots are built at an 8.35% yield on cost and exited in 2031 at a ~6.2% blended cap rate — that development spread, plus land reimbursed at market over raw-dirt basis, is where the return is created.

Return profile, stated plainly: this is a build-and-sell vehicle, not current yield. Preferred return accrues at 8% and pays as operating cash allows (~$6.5M over the hold); approximately 91% of all LP distributions arrive at the 2031 exit. Investors requiring interim income should weigh that profile first.

How the TIF works here

Public capital

A TIF district freezes the tax base at today's near-zero raw-land value. Every dollar of new property and sales tax the development creates — the increment — is captured by the district through 2051. Yukon TIF #3 is projected to generate $351.3M of total increment, of which $193.0M (55%) serves developer-related purposes and the balance remains with the district. After 2051 the district expires and the full tax base flows to the city, county, and schools permanently.

Why the city participates
The increment does not exist without the development. In exchange for sharing taxes that would otherwise never occur, the city receives a $44.9M youth facility, an $8.3M parking garage, and $17.9M of infrastructure — plus the permanent post-2051 tax base.

Costco is the district's engine: it owns its parcel and pays no rent — its contribution is increment, modeled on $160M of annual taxable sales and driving roughly 72% of district TIF. Costco opens 2029; its increment lands in 2030, which shapes the bond structure below. Substantiation of the sales assumption is a pre-closing diligence item (see Diligence status).

Why the bond carries $10.8M of capitalized interest
The buildings that generate the increment don't exist in 2027–29 — native TIF coverage in the trough year is ~0.26x. The bond therefore borrows its own early interest at closing and draws the fund down to hold every year at exactly 1.25x coverage until Costco's increment arrives in 2030, after which the bond is self-supporting from revenue. This is the standard structure for revenue bonds whose revenue source is under construction. It is sized precisely to 1.25x with no additional cushion — a deliberate efficiency with a corresponding risk noted in Section 11.
Why principal is "sculpted"
Repayment isn't level: each year's principal is shaped pro-rata to that year's projected increment — light in 2027–29, heavier after 2030. Total debt service over the life is $178.3M ($75.9M of it interest), fully retired by district end in 2051. The 25-year term exists because the revenue arrives late; a shorter bond could not carry this par.
The 4% reserve figure is built on the assumption of a surety policy in place of a cash-funded debt-service reserve. Obtaining a surety indication through ODFA is confirmation item #3.
What the infrastructure allocation covers
Total infrastructure hard cost is $17.9M. The city's $8.0M frontload and a $0.5M baseball-field donation cover part of it, leaving roughly $9.4M of remaining cost the bond must fund. The allocation is set at $10.5M; the ~$1.1M above hard cost flows to PREF Alpha as equity relief — a disclosed element of the bond sizing.

The cap discipline

Project Plan

TIF assistance to a developer is limited by a Project Plan "not-to-exceed" cap. Because the cap can be measured on different bases, the model computes all three — and the bond was deliberately sized so the structure clears the sponsor's $200M ceiling under every definition, removing the definitional question from the city approval process entirely.

MeasurementAmountQuestion it answers
Assistance principal (par + frontload + TIF-funded incentives)$117,029,020The principal sum of assistance the Plan authorizes — the not-to-exceed ask. Precedent: Pointe Vista ODFA agreement at $110M.

Why debt-service interest isn't "assistance"
The developer never receives the $75.9M of interest — it is paid by the district to bondholders as the cost of monetizing future increment into present dollars. The assistance delivered is the principal sum, which is why the authorization ask ($117.0M) and the district's revenue-budgeting figure ($186.3M) are different numbers answering different questions. Both are disclosed; conflating them double-counts the par.

The Costco land mechanics

Costco receives its 21.12-acre parcel at no cost — the standard inducement for an anchor of this scale. The bond reimburses PREF Alpha at $15/SF fair market value ($13,799,808) for entitled, infrastructure-served commercial land, against a raw-dirt blended basis of ~$4.5M on the parcel. The difference is the return on entitlement, carry, and infrastructure delivery risk the fund took. An independent appraisal supporting the $15/SF is a pre-closing confirmation item.

Additional 2027 dispositions: Yukon Public Schools and the YFAC master developer purchase pads for a combined $3,659,040; proceeds recycle into construction calls.

The 215bp spread is compensation for assembling what no single participant holds: 87 contiguous acres, a first-of-its-kind TIF district for the city, an anchor commitment, $17.9M of fronted infrastructure, and an anchor-grade TI program. If a 2031 sale isn't achievable
The exit is thirteen separately financeable assets, not one buyer. Fallbacks in order: componentized sales (medical office trades in a deep market); refinance at stabilization — an 8.35% yield on cost supports a recapitalization returning most capital while holding; or hold extension, since the portfolio cash-flows above debt service by 2031. A delayed exit largely preserves MOIC while compressing IRR.
